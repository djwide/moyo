# Summary of John F. Kennedy's Assassination

## Basic Facts and Context
- **Date and Location**: John F. Kennedy, the 35th President of the United States, was assassinated on November 22, 1963, in Dallas, Texas, while riding in a presidential motorcade through Dealey Plaza.
- **Event Details**: Kennedy was riding in an open-top 1961 Lincoln Continental limousine with First Lady Jacqueline Kennedy, Texas Governor John Connally, and Connally's wife, Nellie. Governor Connally was also wounded during the assassination.

## The Assassination
- **Time and Shots Fired**: The shooting occurred at approximately 12:30 PM CST. According to the Warren Commission, three shots were fired from the sixth floor of the Texas School Book Depository. Kennedy was struck by two bullets, one in the neck/upper back and a fatal shot to the head.
- **Immediate Aftermath**: Kennedy was rushed to Parkland Memorial Hospital, where he was pronounced dead at 1:00 PM CST. Governor Connally survived his injuries.

## Lee Harvey Oswald
- **Identification as Shooter**: Lee Harvey Oswald, a former U.S. Marine employed at the Texas School Book Depository, was identified as the shooter. He used a Mannlicher-Carcano rifle to fire the shots.
- **Arrest and Death**: Oswald was arrested approximately 70 minutes after the assassination. He was charged with the murder of Kennedy and Dallas police officer J.D. Tippit, whom he allegedly killed shortly after the assassination. On November 24, 1963, Oswald was shot and killed by Jack Ruby, a nightclub owner, while being transferred from the city jail to the county jail. This event was broadcast live on television.

## Investigations and Findings
- **Warren Commission (1964)**: Established by President Lyndon B. Johnson and led by Chief Justice Earl Warren, the commission concluded that Oswald acted alone in the assassination, firing three shots. This remains the official account despite widespread controversy and skepticism.
- **House Select Committee on Assassinations (HSCA, 1979)**: This later investigation suggested that Kennedy was "probably assassinated as a result of a conspiracy," indicating a high probability of a second shooter, but it could not identify specific conspirators.

## Conspiracy Theories
- **Alternative Theories**: Numerous conspiracy theories have been proposed, implicating various groups such as the mafia, CIA, KGB, anti-Castro Cubans, and even suggesting involvement by high-ranking officials like Lyndon B. Johnson. However, these theories remain unproven and speculative.
